Why this project exists

Rice is one of the most water-intensive crops on earth, and traditional flooded cultivation is a significant source of methane - while exposing farmers to erratic rainfall, rising temperatures and declining groundwater. Against that backdrop the project introduced regenerative interventions focused on water, soil and implementation competencies.

Participating farmers kept their prevailing rice establishment method. The single change at the centre of the program was irrigation: AWD replaced continuous flooding with monitored wetting–drying cycles to conserve water and suppress methane formation. Everything else - biologicals, residue management, digital traceability - was built around making that change stick and making it auditable.

The sections that follow set out how each of those interventions was delivered, governed and verified, and what they added up to across the season.

03

Three interventions, one system

Water, soil and residue were addressed together - each supported by the same field team, the same digital record and the same farmer.

Theme 1 · Water

Alternate Wetting & Drying

1 perforated field pipe per acre, read by hand

AWD replaces continuous flooding with a controlled cycle of irrigation and drying. A perforated field water tube is sunk into each acre; irrigation is scheduled by watching the water level fall inside the tube rather than by calendar habit. Kisan Advisors measured levels manually through the season, so farmers learned to read the pipe themselves.

  • ~67% water savings - down to ~1,073 litres per kg of paddy
  • Lower methane emissions through reduced waterlogging
  • Improved root aeration and nutrient uptake
  • Enhanced water productivity and groundwater management
  • Decreased weed pressure in certain field conditions
  • Healthier soil structure - less prolonged saturation stress
  • Farmer-friendly monitoring via a simple tube
  • Climate resilience where irrigation is scarce
Theme 2 · Soil

Oorjit & Grow Phos

6 kg + 20 kg per acre, supplied free of cost

An advanced microbial NPK consortium biofertiliser: nitrogen-fixing, phosphorus-solubilising and potash-mobilising bacteria enriched with naturally derived soil minerals. Every farmer received a 6 kg bag of Oorjit granules and a 20 kg bag of Grow Phos - one acre's worth - plus training on correct application.

  • Steady biologically-driven N-P-K supply; up to 20% less synthetic fertiliser dependence
  • Improved soil structure - aeration, moisture retention, root penetration
  • Lower urea requirement, subject to soil condition, crop stage and agronomic advice
  • Consistent vegetative growth and quality grain formation
  • Lower disease incidence and better long-term soil health
Theme 3 · Residue

Crop Residue Management

833 acres baled against a 300-acre target

CRM was implemented to eliminate open field burning. Selected farmers were supported in baling and bundling rice residues immediately after harvest. The baling process itself was explained to farmers during village-level meetings, ahead of harvest. Where residues are baled and removed, the gains are cleaner fields, avoided burning and productive biomass use.

  • New income stream from selling baled residue to gaushalas
  • Target exceeded - 178% growth over the original 300-acre plan
  • Higher soil organic matter; organic carbon retained rather than burnt
  • Biomass reused as livestock feed, compost and bioenergy
  • Reduced air pollution and fire risk across the project villages

Monitoring, measurement and traceability

Grow Indigo ran a phygital monitoring system: regular field observation paired with digital capture. Farmer information, field boundary geofencing and agronomy records went into FieldKhata; the agronomist and scientific team then checked accuracy, completeness and geolocation consistency before anything reached GHG accounting. Post-harvest, S3 Sutra traced low-emission paddy from farm to miller, and a third-party auditor reviewed the evidence and digital records.

Theme 4 · Four IPM principles, applied in the field

Cultural control. Timely agronomic operations, field sanitation, balanced nutrition, weed management and AWD-based water management.

Mechanical & physical. Removal of infected plant parts, cleaning of field bunds, physical suppression of weeds and pest habitats.

Biological control. Biological inputs and practices that improved soil and crop health and encouraged beneficial organisms.

Chemical control. Recommended only when pest or disease pressure required it - correct pesticide, dosage and crop stage.

A high-touch, phygital extension model

Integrated Nutrient Management. Combined farmer practice, crop-stage requirements, biological inputs, soil condition and split fertiliser application.

Field visits. Kisan Advisors visited from transplanting to harvest for one-on-one support and verification.

Village-level meetings. Held four times, with live demonstrations of AWD, biological application and residue management.

Always-on channels. Vernacular video and weekly WhatsApp messages in Telugu, including pest advisories naming the Kisan Advisor to call.

Stakeholder management

Field teams, scientists, Aishwarya Rice Mills (Nestlé's empanelled miller) and Nestlé representatives worked in a connected framework - enabling timely execution, transparent data flow and high implementation fidelity. The TBM and Kisan Advisors supervised the entire procurement process, and the PMU visited fields to ensure timely completion.

One practice, eight kinds of return

AWD is a climate-smart, water-saving rice cultivation practice that delivers measurable environmental, agronomic, economic and social benefits.

Water conservation

  • Reduces irrigation demand 30–45% vs continuous flooding
  • ~67% water savings achieved (~1,073 L/kg)
  • Preserves groundwater; reduces pumping load

Climate mitigation

  • 771.47 kg CO₂e/MT reduction - ~58% vs baseline
  • Cuts diesel/electric pumping emissions
  • Builds resilience to erratic monsoons

Reduced methane

  • Drying disrupts methanogenic activity
  • 30–70% CH₄ cut per hectare (IRRI, IPCC AR6)
  • Methane ~28× more potent than CO₂ over 100yr

Soil health

  • Wet–dry cycles improve aeration
  • Stronger root systems, better oxygenation
  • Builds soil organic carbon over seasons

Biodiversity

  • Diversifies field micro-habitats
  • Protects pollinators, earthworms, predators
  • Preserves riparian & wetland ecosystems

Energy savings

  • Fewer irrigation events, less pump runtime
  • Lower electricity and diesel use
  • Eases peak agricultural electricity demand

Human health

  • Less standing water limits mosquito breeding
  • Lower nitrate leaching protects wells
  • Ending burning improves air quality

Water governance

  • Low-cost tube enables self-managed water use
  • Supports village/watershed-level planning
  • Creates auditable record for climate finance
The big picture

AWD is a single practice with cascading positive impacts - saving water, reducing emissions, improving soils, protecting biodiversity, cutting energy costs, safeguarding health and strengthening community water governance.

How the nursery stage was handled

Farmers typically raise seedlings on ~10% of their land for ~21 days. With average landholding at ~9.1 acres, that is ~0.9 acres per farmer. Nursery emissions were estimated over ~0.9 acres × ~21 days, then adjusted by a ~33% correction factor.

Nitrogen use optimisation

Application fell from the university-recommended 48 kg N/acre (PJTSAU) to 43.7 kg N/acre - a 9% reduction - driven primarily by Oorjit granules' enhanced fertiliser-use efficiency combined with AWD irrigation. Against farmers' BAU of 62.4 kg N/acre, the project rate is 30% lower.

Evidence indicates AWD enables a further ~13% nitrogen reduction without compromising performance (P.V.M. et al., 2025).

Seedlings were transplanted mid-season and, within the first three days, farmers applied a post-emergence herbicide - Bispyribac Sodium 10SC, or Fenoxaprop-p-ethyl 6.7EC + Metsulfuron Methyl 10WP + Chlorimuron Ethyl 10WP - for early weed suppression. Between 10–15 days after transplanting, AWD field pipes were installed and manual water-level measurement began.

At ~15 DAT (tillering), farmers applied the first split of urea and DAP alongside 6 kg/acre of Oorjit granules and 20 kg of Grow Phos. Around ~55 DAT a second herbicide went on where weed pressure required it. The second urea split followed at ~65 DAT (panicle initiation); the third and final split at ~75 DAT supported grain development.

Post-harvest activity began at crop maturity. Procurement and traceability documentation were completed soon after harvest, with GHG quantification and report submission following.

Insight

AWD alone delivers eight distinct ESG benefits. That breadth lets Nestlé communicate the work credibly across climate, water, biodiversity and rural-development pillars - without overstating any single claim.

Geo-tagged field boundaries, farmer-diary practice records, scientific QC and Cool Farm Platform quantification together produce emission reductions that are field-attributable and third-party verifiable.

The program is a working template for how Responsible Sourcing commitments translate into measurable, defensible field outcomes - scaling climate-aligned procurement across the rice category, and beyond.

Who we are

Grow Indigo partners with rice, cotton and other row-crop farmers to deploy water-saving irrigation, biological soil inputs and residue management practices that cut greenhouse gas emissions without cutting yield. Field teams and Kisan Advisors work season to season with growers on the ground, while every intervention is logged, geofenced and independently quantified - so the outcomes a partner like Nestlé sees in a report like this one are traceable back to a specific farmer, field and season.
